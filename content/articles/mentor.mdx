A mentor helps to grow professionally, to act independently and to cope with challenges.

### 4 principles of mentoring

1. Task definition. <Muted>Mentor will help the mentee to make sense of the work or study process and to set goals and objectives.</Muted>
2. Training and feedback. <Muted>The mentor helps the mentee to learn by doing. He tells and shows how to do it, and then observes and gives feedback.</Muted>
3. Solving complexities and motivating. <Muted>A mentor is usually needed where the mentee enters the field of the unknown and encounters difficulties. The mentor is usually needed where the mentee enters an area of unknowns, encounters difficulties, makes mistakes, doubts his or her abilities or becomes involved in a work conflict.</Muted>
4. Evaluation. <Muted>The mentor assesses the mentee. The mentor himself is also evaluated on the extent to which he has solved all of the mentee's problems and how he has motivated him.</Muted>

### Skills required for a mentor

- Green communication and feedback. <Muted>The mentor communicates a lot with the mentee, observes how the mentee performs tasks and gives regular feedback. Therefore it is important for the mentor to be able to communicate effectively and accurately so that the mentee understands and does not get upset even if things don't work out.</Muted>
- Motivation. <Muted>The mentor is expected to support the mentee. This is why it is important for the mentor to be able to recognise the signs of demotivation, find its causes and help deal with it.</Muted>
- Conflict resolution.

## Feedback

The most important task of feedback is to correct the mentee's behaviour.

Good feedback helps to draw clear conclusions: what was done right or wrong, what was the result of the action and how to behave in the future.

Feedback can't be replaced with instruction. We can't say: I want you to do this, we have to explain the reason and convince the mentee that it is necessary for him or her to do so.

### The structure of feedback

- **action** <Muted>what the person did in a particular situation</Muted> →
- **result** <Muted>what his actions have led to</Muted> →
- **impact** <Muted>it has had an impact to others</Muted> →
- **agreement** <Muted>how he should behave in the future</Muted>

### Content of feedback

For feedback to be effective, it must contain arguments. It should explain cause-effect relationships, if they are not obvious. It should also describe how the action has affected the person giving the feedback, other people and the outcome. When describing the consequences, you can touch on emotions: if the action made someone feel bad, this is also an argument.

By using a question instead of a statement, you can open a dialogue and find a better solution. At the same time it shows the person that you are on the same side as them.

### Feedback Principles

- Approach feedback individually. <Muted>For one, it is important to hear a detailed explanation of what has gone wrong and how to fix it. For another, diving into the finer details will seem like idle chitchat. Some people find it easy to accept critical feedback in any form, but those who respond emotionally will find the choice of words important.</Muted>
- Talk about actions, not qualities of the person. <Muted>If you start commenting on a person's qualities in feedback, this can hurt them personally and cause strong emotions. As a result, you will encounter more resistance and it will be harder to achieve change. Conversely, talking about actions and specific behaviour is less emotionally charged and this feedback is easier for the person to accept.</Muted>
- Avoid assumptions. <Muted>Assuming possible motives for someone's actions and explaining them in your own way is easy to get wrong. The person to whom you are giving feedback may want to correct you, and the focus of the conversation will shift to secondary details.</Muted>
- Give corrective feedback in private.
- Give feedback immediately.
- Maintain a balance of praise and reprimands. <Muted>If it is necessary to give negative feedback, it is essential to monitor how the person is behaving and to praise them for starting to do well.</Muted>

### Toxic feedback

Feedback on emotions, showing things in a negative light.

Toxic feedback violates the principles of respectful communication and has specific objectives.

**Types/Kinds of toxic feedback**

- Active aggression. <Muted>Is an attempt to insult, belittle or verbally attack a person or the result of their work. The aggressor is clearly making it clear that he or she is ready for open conflict.</Muted>
- Passive aggression. <Muted>Is a tendency to express hostility indirectly. All the same attempt to insult, devalue and attack, but without overtly confrontational language.</Muted>
- Comments on a person's qualities, not their actions. <Muted>You should not be allowed to get personal</Muted>
- Feedback is always negative. <Muted>Don't forget to praise your charges.</Muted>
- Breaking personal boundaries.
- Transfer of responsibility. <Muted>Don't blame.</Muted>

### How to solve issues in feedback?

- Reason searching.

  Possible problems:

  - Communication. Conflicting or inaccurate signals.
  - Motivation. Insufficient salary, bad relations in team, family reasons, etc.
  - Skills. Lack of experience/knowledge.
  - Organisation. Blocked by others, requirements changing during the sprint, etc.
  - Culture. Unhealthy team atmosphere.
- Involve observer / mediator. <Muted>A more experienced outsider will be able to spot the problem much more quickly.</Muted>
- Accept or Break up. <Muted>Accept that this person will not change their behaviour on this topic, or not accept it and send them to another team or break up altogether.</Muted>

## Conflicts solving

People cannot agree all the time. Contradictions arise between them - in views, interests or values.

Any conflict has a useful function: it helps to detect a problem and find a better solution.

### Conflict Reasons

- Misunderstanding. <Muted>Establish team rules for communication (develop a template for setting objectives and agree to specify details straight away).</Muted>
- Affected interest. <Muted>The rule of consultative decision-making: if a decision affects the interests of a colleague, it is important to warn him or her about it, to give the opportunity to voice the pros and cons and to propose an alternative.</Muted>
- Different options for solving problems. <Muted>A well-organised recruitment system. If the candidate is introduced to the corporate culture, values and priorities of the team in advance, he or she will have the opportunity to weigh up and decide whether he or she is ready to join and accept the rules of the game.</Muted>
- Organisation of work processes. <Muted>For example the poorly organised booking of the meeting room.</Muted>
- Toxic culture.

### Preparation for conflict-solving conversation

- Main goal. <Muted>Search for something main and positive that will bind together everyone who involved into the conflict.</Muted>
- Position and arguments. <Muted>Politely ask to take into account your arguments. And tell your arguments to others.</Muted>
- Emotions. <Muted>Explain your emotions with I: I fill something negative, instead of You made me fill something negative.</Muted>

**Mediator in conflict**

- Offer help. <Muted>It is important to know that everyone involved into this conflict accept your role as a mediator.</Muted>
- Information gathering. <Muted>Everyone should be heard.</Muted>
- Meeting.

  1. Meeting goal.
  2. Help the parties to articulate the essence of the conflict and outline their common interest.
  3. Discuss the positions of the parties.
  4. Get common solution.
  5. Record the arrangements.
  6. Monitoring plan.
